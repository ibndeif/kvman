# Story and script

Read this before the concept. It applies to every video with words, spoken or on screen.

## The opening

- The first sentence earns the next three seconds. Open with a surprising fact, a question the viewer wants answered, a contrast, the finished result shown first, or the mistake most people make. Vary the kind: don't open every video with a number.
- Never open with "In this video…", "Welcome to…", "Introducing…", or a logo on its own. The brand belongs at the end.
- Within the first 30 seconds, the viewer knows what they will get and why it matters to them.

## The structure

- Link each scene to the next with "but" or "therefore", never "and then": each scene answers the question the one before it raised, or turns it.
- In an explainer, start from what the viewer probably believes, show where it breaks, then give the real mechanism. A corrected belief is remembered better than an answer alone.
- Build the answer so the viewer feels they found it: the question, the obvious attempt and where it fails, the one new idea, then the steps it unlocks.
- One new idea every 30 to 45 seconds at most: one or two in a 60-second video, four to six in a three-minute one.
- The key moment (the reveal, the result, the offer) gets its own scene and a beat of quiet. End that scene's narration on it, and give the scene a `durationMs` 1 to 2 seconds longer than 300 ms plus its narration plus 300 ms, with one slow motion and nothing new entering.
- The ending says the core message in one sentence, calls back to the opening, and gives one next step. No new information there.

## Writing for the ear

- Write as people speak: short sentences, one idea each, contractions where the language has them. Hear it in your head, and rewrite whatever sounds like a brochure.
- Narration and picture say the same thing at the same moment. The narration explains what the picture can't; it never reads the on-screen text aloud, and the on-screen text never repeats a whole spoken sentence.
- The narration's text is what the voice says and what the captions show, so write numbers, symbols, and addresses as they are said, in words, in the video's language: "nearly two trillion dollars", not "$1.9T"; "ten times", not "10x"; "kvman dot app". The scene shows the exact figure. A name the voice gets wrong goes in the pronunciation list.
- Use the real figure from the research rather than "many" or "a lot", and only what a source, the person, or a recording supports.
- Leave room: the narration fills about 85 to 90% of the video, so the opening, the key moment, and the end card can breathe.

## Before you show the treatment

Score the script from 1 to 5 on each of these, and rewrite whatever scores under 3:

- **The opening**: would the viewer stay past three seconds?
- **The length**: within 10% of the word budget (2.5 words a second in English, 2 in Arabic)?
- **The flow**: "but" and "therefore", never "and then"?
- **The picture**: something new on screen every 3 to 5 seconds (every 1 to 3 in a social clip)?
- **The key moment**: does it pay off what the opening promised?
- **The next step**: one, specific, and doable?
