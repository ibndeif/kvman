# 16 — kvvideo (namespace `kvvideo`)

kvvideo makes any kind of video the person needs: a lesson, an explainer, a tutorial of an app or a website, a promotion, an announcement, a story, a social clip (ADR 0115, 55). A video is composed, not generated: websites and HTML pages the agent writes, recorded in the browser already on the computer; the person's own images and clips, and images and clips from the web under a licence that allows their use; a narration from a text-to-speech service or recorded by the person; captions burned into the picture; joined with ffmpeg (ADR 0107, 2 and 3; ADR 0115, 3, 5, 56, and 58). kvvideo has no loop of its own: it extends kvcoder (plan 08 §8.4) with the `video` connector, its guide, and the slash command `/video`. The `coder` preset runs it.

## 16.1 In a chat

- **Off until `/video`** (ADR 0107, 11). kvvideo's `kernel.started` handler registers the `video` connector with `optIn: true`, and the slash command `{ name: 'video', description: 'kvvideo.slash.video', command: 'kvvideo.video.start', message: 'kvvideo.slash.video.message' }`.
- **`kvvideo.video.start { sessionId, argument }`** (public command, `retries: 0`) → `{}`: enables `video` in the chat (`kvcoder.connector.enable`), sets the section `{ id: 'guide', title: 'Making videos', order: 30, sessionId }` from `docs/guide.md`, and, the first time in that chat, adds the note `kvvideo.video.started`.
- **The connector's description**: "Make any kind of video: record websites and HTML pages you write, use the person's images and clips and licensed ones from the web, narrate a script or have the person record it, and compose them with camera moves, transitions, captions, and sound into an MP4. Use it when the person asks for a video of any kind."
- **The guide** (ADR 0115, 1, 23, and 52; ADR 0117) is a production workflow of ten stages, in order, each ending with what it gives the next (a stage the request already settles takes a line), then the production rules, then a few rules. A video's files are in its own folder, such as `videos/<name>/`, with the video beside it.
  - **The stages** (ADR 0117, 1 to 6 and 8 to 17). 1. **Ready**: `video check`, and stop until ffmpeg and the browser are there (below); a missing narration service stops the work only when the video has a generated voice, so with only it missing the lead goes on and settles the voice in the brief (ADR 0119, 1); the person's own voice needs no service (ADR 0120, 4). A generated voice with no service ready: the lead offers to set one up, Piper (installed outside the workspace, with its voice in `check`'s `toolsFolder`) or a service whose key the person adds as a secret, and sets it with `video narration-set` once the person agreed in an `ask` card (ADR 0120, 8 and 26). 2. **Brief**: look first, then ask in one round only what is open, the recommendation first (what the viewer should know or do after watching, who they are, where it is shown, which sets the shapes and length, the length, the language, the voice: generated, the person's own, or none, which is on-screen text and sound effects only), written to `brief.md`. 3. **Research**: the facts with their sources, the person's media, licensed media, and, for an app of the workspace, its flows walked once with their labels, selectors, and clean demo data, added to `brief.md`. 4. **Concept**: the core message in a sentence, the hook of the first 3 s, and a structure from the kind (tutorial, explainer, promotion, announcement, story, social clip); when the concept is open, the worker that debates approaches gives two or three directions, the strongest first, offered with `ask choice`; a tutorial of clear steps or a script the person gave has its concept chosen by the lead. 5. **Treatment**, before anything costs money: a Markdown artifact (the concept, "Look and sound", a row per scene: purpose, narration, picture, on-screen text, motion, transition, length; the web media with sources and licences), `style.css` (colours, fonts, type sizes, spacing, motion) in the video's folder, importing `kvvideo/brand.css` and `kvvideo/motion.css`, and one or two style frames checked with `video look`, drawn and composed into a preview clip of about 6 s that the chat's player shows (ADR 0120, 10); narration of about 2.5 words a second in English and 2 in Arabic, or with no voice each scene long enough to read its on-screen text, the whole within 10% of the length asked; agreed with `ask choice`, which covers what it lists and is the go-ahead for the map; a new brand saved with `brand-set` then. 6. **Production map**, when `execution-map` is listed: voice, scenes, recordings, assembly and review, delivery, parallel where it can be, started at once. 7. **Voice first**: `narrate`, or `ask record` with one card per scene (with examples only when a service is ready) and `voice`; its caption times are the picture's clock, and every scene is re-timed from its measured length, the person asked once (record again, change the words, or accept the length) when the whole is more than 10% from the length asked (ADR 0120, 27); skipped with no voice. 8. **Pictures**: HTML scenes link `style.css` and move with CSS, the motion kit, and scripts on kvvideo's clock (three.js, anime.js, Lottie from `kvvideo/lib/`), each element entering at 300 ms plus its caption line's `startMs`, its sound cued with `data-sound`, each checked with `video look` before it is recorded (ADR 0120, 1, 5, and 11); with four or more HTML scenes besides the style frames, designer workers build them in parallel, one scene file each, briefed (titled motion graphics designer, `maxSteps` at least 30) with `brief.md`, `style.css`, the style frames, the motion rules, the scene's row, its narration, and its caption times, and checked again when they come back (ADR 0120, 29); websites recorded with `atLine`, callouts, highlights, and blurs; images and clips as they are. **With no voice** (ADR 0119, 1 and 2): the on-screen text carries the words, each scene has its `durationMs`, an HTML scene's elements are timed from the recording's start, a website is recorded with `wait` steps between its actions and no `narration` or `atLine`, there are no captions, the sound effects stay, and `review`'s `silence` and `loudness` problems are expected and aren't faults. 9. **Assemble and review**: `compose`, `review` against the treatment (says and shows its row, actions on their words, text spelled right, readable, inside the middle 90%, nothing cut off, overlapping, empty, or badly zoomed, the same look throughout, private data blurred, credits, no problem reported), each fault fixed at its cause through kvvideo's calls and composed again, never by processing the video with `shell` (ADR 0120, 28), at most three rounds, then what is still wrong told. 10. **Deliver**: the other shapes with `extraSizes`, the file, its length, and its shapes told; a change asked at a time is found from the scene times and only that scene is made again.
  - **Story.** Fit the video to its kind, purpose, and audience (a lesson teaches step by step, a promotion leads to its offer, a story has its arc, a social clip hooks in its first seconds): a clear opening that says what the viewer gets, one idea per scene in a logical order, and an ending with the next step or a call to action. Show rather than tell: the narration explains what the picture can't, and on-screen text is a few words. No filler, repetition, or long still shots; scenes of 3 to 12 s unless the content needs more.
  - **Look.** Use the workspace's brand (`video brand`); for the first video take the colours, font, and logo from what the person gave or what the video is about (their app's or site's styles, their logo file), or design them for the video. Keep the colours, type, and style the same in every scene, from `style.css`; design each HTML scene with a clear hierarchy, alignment, contrast, and space, and keep important things inside the middle 90% of the frame. Fill the frame: one focal point per moment, large type, and no half-empty frame. Take the brand's colours, font, and logo from `kvvideo/brand.css` and `kvvideo/logo.<ext>`. Draw titles, diagrams, charts, illustrations, and 3D (three.js) as HTML, CSS, SVG, and canvas pages, and use the person's images and clips and licensed ones from the web for what drawing can't show; never generate images. Show a recorded website in a `window` frame, a phone site in a `phone` frame.
  - **Motion** (ADR 0120, 1). Let `record` and `compose` move the pointer and the camera; in an HTML scene, something always moves: no stretch of 2 s without a change, slow ambient motion under the main elements, entrances eased out over 300 to 600 ms (never linear) and staggered, the key words entering word by word, numbers counting, lines drawing, and the elements leaving before the scene's cut; 3D for a hero moment, not for every scene. Use `crossfade` between scenes, `slide` for a step forward in a sequence, and `fade-black` before the ending.
  - **Text.** Short, correctly spelled, readable on its background, and in the video's language; Arabic text right to left in an Arabic font, with `dir="auto"` on every text element of an HTML scene.
  - **Sound.** Narration, when the video has a voice, in a clear, natural voice whose `tone` and `speed` fit the purpose; names said right through the pronunciation list. Sound effects stay on unless the person asks otherwise: an element's entrance that deserves one has `data-sound`. There is no music. Captions are off when every scene shows its words as large on-screen text, and on for spoken explanations and social clips watched on mute (ADR 0120, 9).
  - **Accuracy and licences.** Say only what is true: what the person gave, what was recorded, or what a source read through a connector says; no invented numbers, features, quotes, or claims. Blur emails, keys, names, and other personal data on screen. A font, library, image, or clip from the web is used only under a licence that allows it, saved in the video's folder, with its source and licence written in the treatment and its credit in the end scene when the licence asks for one; pin a library's exact version.
  - **Rules.** A chat without `ask` (Decide for me, plan 08 §8.1) has its concept chosen by the lead, which shows the treatment, says what it chose, and goes on (ADR 0117, 20), and can't record the person's voice: use the generated one, and say so in the treatment. Narrate a sentence again only when it changed; the cache makes an unchanged one free. When the person asks for a font, find it on the web, save its `.ttf` or `.otf` file in the workspace, and name that file. A target in `record` is a Playwright selector: prefer `role=button[name="Save"]` or `text=Save` to CSS classes.

| `kvvideo.programs.ffmpeg` | `string` (1 to 1000 characters) | `'ffmpeg'` | global |

- **Voice and model.** A `null` voice is the service's default: ElevenLabs `JBFqnCBsd6RMkjVDRZzb`, OpenAI `alloy`; Piper has none, and its voice is the path of a voice's `.onnx` file. A `null` model is `eleven_multilingual_v2` for ElevenLabs and `gpt-4o-mini-tts` for OpenAI; Piper has no model.
- **Pronunciations** (ADR 0115, 15). Each `word` is replaced by its `say` in what is sent to the service, as a whole word (not inside a longer word), ignoring case; the captions keep the text as written.
- **The brand** (ADR 0115, 21 and 43). `logo` is a `.png`, `.svg`, `.jpg`, or `.webp` file in the workspace, or `null`; each colour is `#RRGGBB`; `font` is a bundled font's name, a `.ttf` or `.otf` file in the workspace, or `null`.
- **Programs** (ADR 0107, 20) are a name looked up on the PATH, or a full path.
- **Secrets** (ADR 0107, 21): `elevenlabs.apiKey` and `openai.apiKey`, which the person adds on kvvideo's extension page (plan 06 §6.6). They are read only where a request is made, and never logged, stored elsewhere, or returned.
- **Configuration** (`kvvideo.ui.get`, plan 06 §6.3): four cards, Narration (`service`, `voice`, `model`, `pronunciations`), Brand (`brand`), Recording (`browser`), and Programs (`ffmpeg`, `ffprobe`, `piper`), each a `setting` row. `ui.get` has no pages, nav, panels, or status items.

## 16.3 The `video` connector

| Call | Kernel job | Does |
|---|---|---|
| `video check {}` | `kvvideo.setup.get` (query) | what is ready (§16.4) |
| `video fonts {}` | `kvvideo.font.list` (query) | the bundled fonts: `[{ name, scripts: ('latin' \| 'arabic')[] }]`, by name |
| `video brand {}` | `kvvideo.brand.get` (query) | the workspace's `kvvideo.brand` |
| `video brand-set { logo, colors, font }` | `kvvideo.brand.set` | sets the workspace's `kvvideo.brand`, answers `{}` |
| `video narration-set { service, voice?, model? }` | `kvvideo.narration.set` | sets the narration service (§16.4), answers `{}` |
| `video record { url \| page, steps?, file, width?, height?, device?, narration?, replace? }` | `kvvideo.clip.record` | records a website or a page (§16.5) |
| `video look { url \| page, atMs?, width?, height?, device? }` | `kvvideo.frame.capture`, `images: true` | shows a website's or a page's frames at chosen moments (§16.5 Looking) |
| `video narrate { text, file, tone?, speed?, replace? }` | `kvvideo.narration.make` | narrates text (§16.6) |
| `video voice { lines, file, clean?, replace? }` | `kvvideo.voice.make` | makes a narration from the person's takes (§16.7) |
| `video compose { file, scenes, width?, height?, extraSizes?, captions?, direction?, sounds?, replace? }` | `kvvideo.video.compose`, card `kvvideo.player` | composes the scenes into an MP4 (§16.8) |
| `video review { file }` | `kvvideo.video.review`, `images: true` | measures a video and shows its frames (§16.10) |

- Every job is public and has `retries: 0` (ADR 0107, 16). Timeouts: `record` 10 minutes, `look` 2, `narrate` and `voice` 5, `compose` 60, `review` 10, and `check`, `fonts`, `brand`, `brand-set`, and `narration-set` 1.
- None asks the person (ADR 0107, 17; ADR 0115, 43). A read-only worker's helper has `check`, `fonts`, and `brand`, the queries (plan 08 §8.5).
- Each command has an example in its connector entry.
- **Progress** (ADR 0115, 46 and 54). `record`, `narrate`, `voice`, `compose`, and `review` send progress chunks `{ key, params? }` as they go, `key` a key of kvvideo's catalog with `en` and `ar` texts: `kvvideo.progress.step` ("Recording step {n} of {of}"), `.frame` ("Drawing frame {n} of {of}"), `.sentence` ("Narrating sentence {n} of {of}"), `.take` ("Cleaning take {n} of {of}"), `.scene` ("Scene {n} of {of}"), `.final` ("Final pass, {percent}%"), and `.sheet` ("Sheet {n} of {of}").

**Paths.** Every `file`, `page`, `clip`, `narration`, `take`, `background`, `logo`, and `font` resolves against the workspace folder and must stay inside it, through `..` or a symlink (`VALIDATION_FAILED`). An input that doesn't exist fails `NOT_FOUND`. An output whose extension isn't its command's (`.mp4` for `record` and `compose`, `.mp3` for `narrate` and `voice`, in any case) fails `VALIDATION_FAILED`, and so does one that exists when `replace` isn't `true`. An output's parent folders are created; it is written beside its path under a temporary name and renamed over it, so a failed call leaves no file and the old one in place. The files a command writes beside its output (its sidecar, and `compose`'s `.srt`, poster, and other sizes) follow the same rules and are replaced with it.

**The sidecar** (ADR 0115, 31). `record`, `narrate`, `voice`, and `compose` write `<file>.kvvideo.json` beside their output: `record` `{ kind: 'site' | 'page', source, width, height, device, scale: 2, durationMs, steps, actions, drawn, sounds }` (`source` the `url` or `page`, `steps` the call's steps, `actions` its answer's steps, `drawn` whether it was drawn frame by frame, `sounds` its sound cues, §16.5), `narrate` and `voice` `{ kind: 'narration', durationMs, captions }`, and `compose` `{ kind: 'video', durationMs, width, height, scenes: [{ startMs, endMs }], captions }` (on the video's time line). A command that reads one validates it with zod, and treats a missing or invalid one as absent.

**Programs.** A program runs with an empty stdin, in its own process group on Linux and macOS, and its tree is killed when the job is cancelled or times out: the group on Linux and macOS, `taskkill /PID <pid> /T /F` on Windows. A program that can't be started fails `kvvideo/PROGRAM_MISSING { program, setting }`, where `program` is `ffmpeg`, `ffprobe`, or `piper` and `setting` its key.

## 16.4 `check`, `fonts`, and the brand

`kvvideo.setup.get {}` → `{ ffmpeg, browser, narration }`:

- `ffmpeg: { ready, version, missing }`: runs `<ffmpeg> -hide_banner -version`, `-encoders`, and `-filters`, and `<ffprobe> -hide_banner -version`, 5 s each. `version` is the third word of ffmpeg's first line, or `null`. `missing` lists, in this order, what isn't there: `ffmpeg`, `ffprobe`, the encoders `libx264`, `aac`, and `libmp3lame`, and the filters `subtitles` (ffmpeg built with libass), `xfade`, `loudnorm`, `afftdn`, `gradients`, `perspective`, `alphamerge`, `drawtext` (ffmpeg built with libfreetype), and `xstack`. `ready` is `missing` being empty.
- `browser: { ready, channel }`: starts the browser of `kvvideo.browser` headless and closes it: `auto` tries `chrome`, then `msedge`. `channel` is the one that started, or `null`.
- `narration: { service, ready, missing, toolsFolder }`: `missing` is the first of: `kvvideo.narration.service` when it is `null`; the secret `elevenlabs.apiKey` or `openai.apiKey` that the service needs and that isn't set; `piper` when `<piper> --help` doesn't exit 0 in 5 s; `kvvideo.narration.voice` when Piper's is `null`. It is `null` when nothing is missing, and `ready` is `missing` being `null`. No service is called. A video in the person's own voice needs no service (ADR 0120, 4). `toolsFolder` is `<KVMAN_HOME>/kvvideo`, where Piper and its voices are installed, or `null` when kvman has no home (ADR 0120, 26).

`kvvideo.font.list {}` lists the bundled fonts (ADR 0107, 24): `Amiri`, `IBM Plex Sans Arabic`, `Lato`, and `Poppins`; the two Arabic ones have both scripts.

`kvvideo.narration.set { service, voice?, model? }` → `{}` (ADR 0120, 8 and 26) sets the global `kvvideo.narration.service` to `service` (`'elevenlabs'`, `'openai'`, or `'piper'`), and `.voice` and `.model` to theirs, `null` when left out. Piper's `voice` is required: a full path to an existing `.onnx` file with its `.onnx.json` beside it, anywhere on the computer; ElevenLabs's and OpenAI's is a voice id of 1 to 500 characters, and Piper has no `model` (`VALIDATION_FAILED` otherwise). The guide calls it only after the person agreed in an `ask` card.

`kvvideo.brand.get {}` → the workspace's `kvvideo.brand`, or `null`. `kvvideo.brand.set { logo, colors, font }` checks the brand's files (§16.3 Paths) and a font's name (§16.8 Captions), and sets the workspace's `kvvideo.brand`.

## 16.5 `record`

`kvvideo.clip.record { url | page, steps?, file, width?, height?, device?, narration?, replace? }` → `{ file, durationMs, steps: [{ do, atMs, actMs?, endMs, box? }], sounds: [{ sound, atMs }], failed: [{ url, status }] }`. It needs ffmpeg as `compose` does. Exactly one of `url` and `page` is given (`VALIDATION_FAILED` otherwise).

- **A website** (ADR 0115, 56). `url`, and the `url` of each `goto`, is any http or https address with no user name or password; anything else fails `VALIDATION_FAILED`. Each call's browser is fresh, with no saved sign-ins or cookies.
- **A page** (ADR 0115, 3, 4, and 32). `page` is an `.html` or `.htm` file in the workspace. It is opened at `http://page.kvvideo.invalid/<its file name>`, and each request to that origin is answered with the file at that path under the page's folder, with the content type of its extension; a path that leaves the folder, or a file that doesn't exist, is answered 404. A page may load anything from the internet; the browser's own local-network protection refuses its requests to this computer and the local network (ADR 0115, 59). A `goto` in a page's steps fails `VALIDATION_FAILED`.
- **kvvideo's address in a page** (ADR 0120, 6, 7, 18, and 19). A request to `kvvideo/` under the page's origin is answered by kvvideo, never from the page's folder: `kvvideo/motion.css`, the motion kit (§16.5 The motion kit); `kvvideo/brand.css`, the workspace's brand as `:root { --brand-primary; --brand-background; --brand-text; --brand-font }` (with no brand `#2563EB`, `#0F172A`, `#F8FAFC`, and `'IBM Plex Sans Arabic'` for a page whose `lang` starts with `ar`, else `'Poppins'`), an `@font-face` for each bundled font by its name (`kvvideo/fonts/<file>`, any weight), and, for a brand whose font is a file of the workspace, one by its family name at `kvvideo/fonts/brand.<its extension>`; `kvvideo/logo.<ext>`, the brand's logo, with its own extension; `kvvideo/fonts/<file>`, the bundled font files, and `kvvideo/fonts/brand.<ext>`, the brand's font file; `kvvideo/lib/three/<path>`, three.js's files under its `build/` and `examples/jsm/` folders; `kvvideo/lib/anime.js`, anime.js's ES module bundle; and `kvvideo/lib/lottie.js`, lottie-web's player. Anything else under `kvvideo/`, and the logo with no brand logo, is answered 404. A website gets none of this.
- **Failed loads** (ADR 0120, 7 and 20). The answer's `failed` lists the requests of the website or the page that got an HTTP status of 400 or more (`status`) or failed (`status` 0), with their full address, in the order they ended, without repeats, at most 20; aborted requests to kvman's own address are left out.
- **kvman's own address** (ADR 0107, 18), the port in `<KVMAN_HOME>/kvman.lock` on `localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, or `[::1]`, fails `VALIDATION_FAILED` as a `url`, and every request the recorded page makes to that port is aborted whatever its host, since other names of this computer (`0.0.0.0`, `127.0.0.2`, `[::ffff:127.0.0.1]`) reach kvman too.
- **Steps** (0 to 200), each an object with `do`, and each but `wait` may have `atLine` (§16.5 Narration):

  | Step | Does |
  |---|---|
  | `{ do: 'goto', url }` | opens the address and waits for its load |
  | `{ do: 'click', target }` | moves to the target and clicks it (taps it on a phone) |
  | `{ do: 'type', target, text }` | moves to the target, clicks it, and types `text` (1 to 2000 characters) |
  | `{ do: 'press', key }` | presses a key or a combination, as `Enter` or `Control+A` |
  | `{ do: 'hover', target }` | moves to the target |
  | `{ do: 'scroll', pixels }` | scrolls down by `pixels` (−20 000 to 20 000; negative scrolls up) |
  | `{ do: 'wait', ms }` | holds the picture for `ms` (0 to 60 000) |
  | `{ do: 'waitFor', target }` | waits until the target is visible |

  A `target` is a Playwright selector (CSS, `text=Save`, or `role=button[name="Save"]`, 1 to 500 characters), and its first match is used once visible, within 10 s.
- **The page's size** (ADR 0115, 8 and 35). `device` is `'desktop'` (the default) or `'phone'`. The viewport is `width` × `height` CSS pixels (each even, 320 to 1920): 1280 × 720 by default on a desktop, and 390 × 844 on a phone, which is a touch screen. The browser is started with `--force-device-scale-factor=2` and the page drawn at 2× density, so the file is `2 × width` by `2 × height`.
- **The picture.** Once the page has loaded, all its fonts are loaded (`document.fonts.ready`), and the network has been idle for 500 ms (at most 10 s), the page is shown 500 ms before the first step, 400 ms after each step but `wait` and `waitFor`, and 1000 ms after the last. Scroll bars are hidden.
- **The page's clock** (ADR 0117, 5 and 15). From before the page loads until the capture starts, the page's CSS animations and transitions, and every other animation of its document, are held at their start; they run from the moment the capture starts, so a page's time 0 is the recording's start, whatever the wait for fonts and the network took. In a page recorded in real time, timers of the page's scripts are not held.
- **Drawn frame by frame** (ADR 0120, 2, 13 to 16). A page whose steps are all `wait`, or that has none, is drawn frame by frame on kvvideo's clock rather than recorded in real time: its length is what the real-time rules would give (500 ms before the first step, the steps, 1000 ms after the last, at least the narration's 300 ms, length, and 300 ms), and for each frame *n* from 0, while *n* × 1000 ÷ 60 is below that length, the clock is set to that time and the page is shot as a JPEG of quality 92 at its 2× density and piped to `<ffmpeg> -f image2pipe -framerate 60 -c:v mjpeg -i -`, encoded as the real-time capture is but at 60 frames a second. `durationMs` is the frames' count × 1000 ÷ 60, rounded. A progress chunk `kvvideo.progress.frame` is sent every 30 frames. Its sidecar's `drawn` is `true`.
- **kvvideo's clock** (ADR 0120, 5, 16, and 17). In a page drawn frame by frame, and in `look` of a page, a script given to the page before its own replaces `requestAnimationFrame`, `cancelAnimationFrame`, `performance.now`, `Date.now` and `new Date()` with no argument, `setTimeout`, `setInterval`, `clearTimeout`, and `clearInterval` with ones on a clock that starts at 0 and moves only when kvvideo sets it, and `Math.random` with a generator seeded the same at every load. Setting the clock to *t* runs, in time order, each timer due by *t* at its own time (an interval again each period), then each frame callback requested before, once, with *t*; then every animation of the document (CSS animations and transitions, and the page's own) is paused and set to *t − b*, *b* the clock's time when it was first seen, and each `<video>` and `<audio>` element is paused and set to its own time the same way. The page's clock starts when the frames start.
- **Sound cues** (ADR 0120, 11 and 21). In a page drawn frame by frame, an element with `data-sound` of `pop`, `whoosh`, or `click` is cued at the first frame at which one of its own animations has passed its delay, once; any other value is ignored. The answer's `sounds` lists the cues `{ sound, atMs }` in time order, and is empty for a recording in real time.
- **The motion kit** (ADR 0120, 1), `kvvideo/motion.css`, bundled with kvvideo: easing and duration variables; entrances (`kv-fade`, `kv-rise`, `kv-drop`, `kv-slide` from the reading direction's start, `kv-zoom`, `kv-blur`, `kv-wipe`, `kv-pop`), each starting at the element's `--at` (a time, `0s` by default); an exit, `kv-out`, at its `--out`; emphasis (`kv-pulse`, `kv-shine`, `kv-underline`); ambient motion that loops for the scene (`kv-float`, `kv-breathe`, `kv-drift`, `kv-spin`); `kv-words`, whose children enter one after another, each `--stagger` (80 ms by default) after the last, by their `--i`; `kv-draw`, an SVG shape with `pathLength="1"` drawn over its `--length`; `kv-count`, a number counting from `--from` to `--to`; and `kv-stage`, a frame-filling scene with a safe margin of 5%. A `dir="rtl"` page mirrors the moves along the line.
- **Motion** (ADR 0115, 9). Before it acts on a target, the pointer moves to the target's center along a gentle curve, easing in and out, over 300 ms plus 0.6 ms per pixel of distance, from 400 to 900 ms. On a desktop the page draws an arrow pointer (white, with a dark outline) that follows the mouse, hidden until a step first moves it (ADR 0115, 53); a press shrinks it to 85% for 120 ms and shows a ring that grows and fades over 500 ms. On a phone the page draws a round touch mark at each tap instead. `type` types each character after 35 to 110 ms, with 150 ms more after a space or a punctuation mark, from a sequence seeded by the text, so a recording is the same each time. `scroll` scrolls smoothly, easing in and out, over 400 ms plus 0.5 ms per pixel, at most 3 s.
- **Narration** (ADR 0115, 16 and 41). `narration` is an audio file with a sidecar from `narrate` or `voice` (`VALIDATION_FAILED` without one). A step with `atLine: n` (from 1, at most the number of caption lines, `VALIDATION_FAILED` otherwise) starts no earlier than 300 ms plus line `n`'s `startMs` after the recording's start, and at once when that time has passed. The recording lasts at least 300 ms plus the narration's length plus 300 ms, holding the last picture. A step with `atLine` and no `narration` fails `VALIDATION_FAILED`.
- **The answer** (ADR 0115, 33). `durationMs` is the recording's length. Each step answers `atMs`, when it started; `actMs`, when the pointer arrived and the click, typing, or hover began (`click`, `type`, and `hover`); `endMs`, when it finished; and `box`, `{ x, y, width, height }` in CSS pixels of the viewport, the target's box when it acted (`click`, `type`, and `hover`). All are from the recording's start.
- **Failures.** A page that doesn't load fails `kvvideo/STEP_FAILED { step: 0, reason }`, and a step that fails, `{ step: <its number from 1>, reason }`, where `reason` is the first line of the browser's message. No browser fails `kvvideo/BROWSER_MISSING { channels }`, the channels it tried. Nothing is written when it fails.
- **The capture** (ADR 0107, 25), for a website and a page recorded in real time. Once the page is ready, kvvideo starts the page's screencast (JPEG frames at quality 95, at most the file's size) and pipes the frames to `<ffmpeg> -f image2pipe -framerate 30 -c:v mjpeg -i -`, which writes the file as H.264 (`libx264 -preset veryfast -crf 14`, `yuv420p`, scaled and padded to the file's size, no sound, `+faststart`). The screencast sends a frame only when the picture changes, so each frame is written again until the next one comes, one copy every 1/30 s; the last is written until the recording ends. The recording's start, its length, and each step's times are all on the same clock. An ffmpeg exit other than 0 fails `kvvideo/RENDER_FAILED { reason }` with the last 20 lines of its output.
- The browser is started headless for the call and closed when it ends, fails, or is cancelled, and ffmpeg is stopped with it.
- **Looking** (ADR 0117, 4 and 19; ADR 0120, 17 and 20). `kvvideo.frame.capture { url | page, atMs?, width?, height?, device? }` → `{ images: [{ fileId, name, atMs }], failed: [{ url, status }] }` opens a website or a page as `record` does (its addresses, its routing, kvman's own address, its size and device, its readiness, and its held clock), with no steps, no pointer, and no capture, and writes nothing in the workspace. `atMs` is 1 to 6 distinct moments, each 0 to 60 000 ms from the clock's start (`[1000]` when left out; `VALIDATION_FAILED` otherwise), taken in increasing order. At each moment, a website's animations are all set to that time, and a page's clock (kvvideo's clock) is moved to it in steps of 1/60 s, so a frame is the same at every call; then the page is shot as a JPEG (quality 85) at the viewport's CSS size. The frames are stored in the kernel's files as `look-<n>.jpg` and listed in `images`, in increasing `atMs`, which the model sees (plan 08 §8.4). kvvideo's workspace collection `lookFrames` holds `{ fileId, createdAt }`; past 24, the oldest are deleted with their files. A page that doesn't load fails `kvvideo/STEP_FAILED { step: 0, reason }`, and no browser `kvvideo/BROWSER_MISSING { channels }`. It needs no ffmpeg, and has no progress chunks.

## 16.6 `narrate`

`kvvideo.narration.make { text, file, tone?, speed?, replace? }` → `{ file, durationMs, captions: [{ text, startMs, endMs, words? }], reused }`.

- `text` is 1 to 4000 characters; `tone` 1 to 300 characters; `speed` 0.7 to 1.2 (1 by default). A `null` service fails `kvvideo/NARRATION_NOT_SET { setting: 'kvvideo.narration.service' }`, and Piper with a `null` voice `{ setting: 'kvvideo.narration.voice' }`. A missing key fails `kvvideo/NARRATION_KEY_MISSING { service, secret }`.
- **Sentence by sentence** (ADR 0115, 17 and 41). The text is cut into sentences as the captions are (below), and each sentence is spoken alone, with `kvvideo.narration.pronunciations` applied. The sentences are joined in order with 250 ms of silence between them, and encoded as MP3 (`libmp3lame`, 192 kb/s).
- **ElevenLabs**: `POST https://api.elevenlabs.io/v1/text-to-speech/<voice>/with-timestamps?output_format=mp3_44100_128` with the header `xi-api-key` and `{ text, model_id, previous_text?, next_text?, voice_settings?: { speed } }`: `previous_text` and `next_text` are the sentences before and after it, and `voice_settings` is sent when `speed` isn't 1. The answer is `{ audio_base64, alignment: { characters, character_start_times_seconds, character_end_times_seconds } }`.
- **OpenAI**: `POST https://api.openai.com/v1/audio/speech` with `Authorization: Bearer <key>` and `{ model, voice, input, response_format: 'mp3', instructions?, speed? }`: `instructions` is `tone`, and `speed` is sent when it isn't 1.
- **Piper**: `<piper> -m <voice> -f <temporary .wav> --length-scale <1 ÷ speed> -- <sentence>`. `tone` is used by OpenAI only (ADR 0115, 39).
- A request gets 60 s. An answer other than 2xx fails `kvvideo/NARRATION_FAILED { service, status, reason }` with the first 300 characters of its body as `reason`; a request that fails or passes its 60 s fails `{ service, reason }`. Piper's exit other than 0 fails `{ service: 'piper', reason }` with the last 20 lines of its output.
- **The cache** (ADR 0115, 40). kvvideo's workspace collection `narrations` holds `{ key, fileId, durationMs, alignment, createdAt }` (index `key`), the audio in the kernel's files. `key` is the SHA-256 of the JSON of the service, voice, model, `tone`, `speed`, and the sentence as spoken. A sentence with a row is taken from it and not sent; one without is sent and stored. Past 2000 rows, the oldest are deleted with their files. `reused` is the number of sentences taken from the cache.
- `durationMs` is the file's length, read with `<ffprobe> -v error -show_entries format=duration -of csv=p=0`.
- **Captions** (ADR 0107, 7; ADR 0115, 17). The text is cut at line breaks, and runs of white space within a line become one space. Each line is cut into sentences after `.`, `!`, `?`, `؟`, and `…` followed by a space; empty ones are dropped. A sentence over 42 characters is cut into the fewest lines of whole words of at most 42 characters, as even in length as the words allow (the longest line as short as it can be), breaking after a comma, `،`, a semicolon, or a colon instead when a break there keeps every line within 42 characters and the same number of lines (ADR 0120, 24); a word over 42 characters is a line of its own. A sentence's lines cover its own audio: with ElevenLabs each line starts at its first character's time and each word (`words`, `{ text, startMs, endMs }`) runs from its first character's start to its last character's end, a word replaced by a pronunciation taking the time of what was said in its place; with the other services each line lasts the sentence's length × its characters ÷ the sentence's characters. Lines follow each other with no gap, a sentence's last line ends where the next sentence starts, times are whole milliseconds rounded down, and the last ends at `durationMs`.

## 16.7 `voice`

`kvvideo.voice.make { lines: [{ take, text }], file, clean?, replace? }` → `{ file, durationMs, captions }` (ADR 0115, 28 and 50).

- `lines` is 1 to 20, each `take` an audio file (`.webm`, `.ogg`, `.m4a`, `.mp4`, `.mp3`, `.wav`), as `ask record` wrote it (plan 08 §8.5), and its `text` the sentence said in it (1 to 500 characters).
- **The cleanup**, with `clean` `true` (the default), each take through `highpass=f=80`, `afftdn=nr=12:nf=-30`, trimming silence below −45 dB at both ends down to 150 ms, and `loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5:LRA=11`; with `clean` `false`, each take is only converted. The takes are never changed.
- The cleaned takes are joined in order with 250 ms of silence between them and encoded as MP3 (`libmp3lame`, 192 kb/s). Each take's `text` is cut into sentences and each sentence into lines as §16.6 cuts them; the take's sentences share its length in proportion to their characters, each sentence's lines likewise, and a sentence's captions never join the next one's (ADR 0120, 24).
- An ffmpeg exit other than 0 fails `kvvideo/RENDER_FAILED { take, reason }` (`take` from 1, or `null` while joining).

## 16.8 `compose`

`kvvideo.video.compose { file, scenes, width?, height?, extraSizes?, captions?, direction?, sounds?, replace? }` → `{ file, durationMs, bytes, fileId, poster, posterFileId, others: [{ file, bytes }] }`.

- **Scenes** (1 to 100), each `{ clip, from?, to?, narration?, captions?, durationMs?, frame?, background?, camera?, overlays?, transition?, sounds? }`:
  - `clip`: a video (`.webm`, `.mp4`, `.mov`, `.mkv`) or an image (`.png`, `.jpg`, `.jpeg`, `.webp`);
  - `from` and `to`: where the video's part starts and ends, in milliseconds (`to` after `from`; an image's fail `VALIDATION_FAILED`);
  - `narration`: an audio file (`.mp3`, `.wav`, `.m4a`);
  - `captions`: up to 200 `{ text, startMs, endMs, words? }` from the narration's start (`text` 1 to 200 characters, `endMs` after `startMs`, up to 40 `words`); left out, the narration's sidecar's;
  - `durationMs`: 100 to 600 000;
  - `frame`: `'window'`, `'phone'`, or `'none'`; left out, `window` for a recording of a website on a desktop, `phone` for one on a phone, else `none`;
  - `background`: `#RRGGBB` or an image file; left out, the brand's;
  - `camera`: `'auto'` or `'none'`; left out, `auto` for a recording of a website or a page and for an image, else `none` (any other video clip is never moved);
  - `overlays`: up to 20 (§16.8 Overlays);
  - `transition`: into the next scene, `{ kind, toward?, ms? }` (§16.8 Transitions);
  - `sounds`: `false` turns the scene's sound effects off.
- **A scene's length** (ADR 0115, 41) is its `durationMs`; else 300 ms plus its narration's length plus 300 ms; else its video part's length. An image with neither fails `VALIDATION_FAILED`. The narration starts 300 ms into the scene. A video part shorter than the scene holds its last frame; a longer one is cut.
- **The picture** is `width` × `height`, each even, 320 to 1920 (1920 × 1080 by default), at 60 frames a second (ADR 0120, 12); a clip of fewer frames a second is shown at 60 by repeating its frames. `direction` is `'ltr'` or `'rtl'`; left out, `rtl` when a caption has an Arabic letter, else `ltr`.
- **The camera** (ADR 0115, 7 and 34), with `auto`, from the clip's sidecar's `actions`: each `click` or `type` step, with its `box`, is a stop. The view zooms to 1.6× (to `0.8 ×` the viewport ÷ the box when that is smaller, and never below 1), centred on the box's center and kept inside the page. It starts easing in at the step's `atMs` and is there at its `actMs`, or 400 ms after `atMs` when that is later (a tap on a phone acts at once); a stop whose `atMs` is within 2.5 s of the last stop's `endMs` is reached by panning, eased, over 600 ms; 1.5 s after the last stop's `endMs` of a run, the view eases out over 800 ms. The easing is cubic in and out. The view is drawn with ffmpeg's `perspective`, evaluated each frame, so it moves by fractions of a pixel, and a scene's filter graph is given to ffmpeg as a file (`-filter_complex_script`). A clip without a sidecar has no stops.
- **An image's camera** (ADR 0115, 58), with `auto`: the view zooms from 1× to 1.08× over the scene, at an even pace, centred, so the picture never sits still.
- **A page's camera** (ADR 0120, 1), with `auto`, for a recording of a page: a slow push-in from 1× to 1.05× over the scene, at an even pace, centred.
- **The frame** (ADR 0115, 10 and 36). Behind the clip is the `background`: an image fills the frame, cropped; a colour, or the brand's `background` without one, is a diagonal gradient from it to the same colour mixed with 18% of the brand's `primary`; with no brand, from `#0F172A` to `#1E293B`. `window`: the clip in rounded corners (1.5% of the frame's shorter side) with a soft shadow, as large as fits inside margins of 6% of the shorter side, and 14% at the bottom when the captions are at the bottom, so the captions sit on the background. `phone`: the clip in a dark phone outline, 88% of the frame's height. `none`: the clip as large as fits, on the background. The background is drawn by ffmpeg (`gradients` with no motion, a colour, or the image); the shadow, the corners, and the outline are drawn once per scene by the browser as images at the picture's size, and ffmpeg places the clip in them, so a scene framed `none` needs no browser (ADR 0115, 60).
- **Overlays** (ADR 0115, 11), each `{ kind, step?, box?, text?, fromMs, toMs }`: `kind` is `'callout'`, `'highlight'`, or `'blur'`; exactly one of `step` (a step of the clip's sidecar, from 1, which must have a `box`) and `box` (`{ x, y, width, height }` in CSS pixels of the recorded page) places it (`VALIDATION_FAILED` otherwise); `fromMs` and `toMs` are from the scene's start (`toMs` after `fromMs`). `callout` has `text` (1 to 80 characters, required): a rounded label in the brand's `primary` colour (`#FFD54A` with no brand, as the captions' highlight), its text white or near-black, whichever contrasts more, in the brand's font or the captions' default, with an arrow to the box, above it when there is room and else below; it fades in and out over 200 ms. `highlight` dims everything but the box (12 px more on each side, rounded) to 55% black. `blur` blurs the box with a strength of 20 px. Overlays are applied to the page, before the camera and the frame, and a callout is drawn as an image by the browser.
- **Captions** are burned in (ADR 0107, 6; ADR 0115, 18 and 42), from an ASS file per scene, centered, each line in the direction of its own text (the style's encoding is -1, so an Arabic line reads right to left), one line at a time. `captions.style`: `'boxed'` (text on a box with padding of a third of the text's size: with a brand, the box in its `primary` at 90% opacity and the text white or near black (`#111111`), whichever contrasts more; with no brand, white on black at 65% opacity; the default with a brand, ADR 0120, 9),  `'outline'` (white text with a black outline of 3 px; the default with no brand), `'highlight'` (as `boxed`, and the words already spoken coloured in the brand's `primary`, or `#FFD54A` with no brand, for a line with `words`; a line without is `boxed`), or `'off'` (no captions). `captions.position` is `bottom` (the default) or `top`, 6% of the height from the edge, at `captions.size` px (12 to 200, the height ÷ 18 by default). `captions.font` is a bundled font's name or a `.ttf` or `.otf` file in the workspace, named by its family from the file's own name table; left out, the brand's font, else IBM Plex Sans Arabic when a caption has an Arabic letter, else Poppins (ADR 0107, 24). An unknown name fails `VALIDATION_FAILED` with the bundled names. The characters `{`, `}`, and `\` are left out of a caption.
- **Transitions** (ADR 0115, 12). `kind` is `'cut'`, `'crossfade'` (the default), `'fade-black'`, or `'slide'`; `ms` is 0 to 2000 (500 by default, or half the shorter of the two scenes when that is less; 0 for a cut), and at most half of each of the two scenes' lengths (`VALIDATION_FAILED` otherwise). A `slide` has `toward`: `'start'` (the default), `'end'`, `'up'`, or `'down'`; `start` is left in an `ltr` video and right in an `rtl` one. A transition overlaps the two scenes by its `ms`, and their sound crossfades over the same time. The last scene's `transition` fails `VALIDATION_FAILED`.
- **Sound effects** (ADR 0115, 14 and 38; ADR 0120, 11 and 21), unless `sounds` is `false` for the video or the scene: a click at each `click` step's `actMs`; key taps from each `type` step's `actMs` plus 120 ms to its `endMs`; a whoosh at each `slide`, and a soft whoosh at each `crossfade`; a pop as each callout appears; and each sound cue of a drawn page's sidecar at its `atMs`, within the scene's part. They are bundled with kvvideo, made by it with ffmpeg and released under CC0, with a `SOURCES.md` giving each file's command and licence (ADR 0115, 62), and mixed 18 dB below the narration (24 dB for the key taps, 26 dB for a crossfade's whoosh).
- **The render** (ADR 0107, 23; ADR 0115, 2 and 37). ffmpeg runs in a temporary folder, where each scene's ASS file, the caption font, and the drawn images are, so filters name them by their relative paths. Each scene becomes a part there, at 60 frames a second: H.264 (`libx264`, `-preset veryfast -crf 14`, `yuv420p`) with PCM sound (48 kHz, stereo), its narration placed at 300 ms or silence, with 20 ms fades at both ends, in a `.mkv`. The final pass joins the parts with `xfade` and `acrossfade` (a cut is a transition of 0 ms), mixes the sound effects, measures the loudness with `loudnorm` (`I=-14:TP=-2:LRA=11`, printing JSON, so the encoded sound stays at or below −1 dBTP, ADR 0120, 22), and then encodes once with the measured values (`linear=true`; a silent mix, measured at `-inf`, is left as it is): H.264 (`libx264`, `-preset medium -crf 18`, `yuv420p`) with AAC sound (192 kb/s, 48 kHz, stereo), `+faststart` (ADR 0115, 19). The temporary folder is deleted when the call ends, in every case. An ffmpeg exit other than 0 fails `kvvideo/RENDER_FAILED { scene, reason }` (`scene` from 1, or `null` in the final pass) with the last 20 lines of its output.
- **Beside the video.** No `.srt` is written (ADR 0117, 7). The poster `<name>.jpg` is the frame in the middle of the first scene, 1280 px on its longer side, JPEG quality 90 (ADR 0115, 45); the sidecar is §16.3's.
- **Other sizes** (ADR 0115, 44). `extraSizes` is up to 2 `{ width, height }`, each as `width` and `height`, each different from the main size and from each other (`VALIDATION_FAILED` otherwise). Each is composed from the same scenes into `<name>-<width>x<height>.mp4`, with its own poster, and listed in `others`. A scene whose clip is a recorded page with a different shape is recorded again for it, in the temporary folder, with the same steps, at that shape with its shorter side 720 CSS pixels; this needs the browser, and fails `kvvideo/BROWSER_MISSING` without one.
- **The answer.** `durationMs` is the video's length, the scenes' lengths less their transitions' overlaps, and `bytes` the file's size. `poster` is the poster's path.
- **The player's copy** (ADR 0107, 13 and 14). A file of at most 512 MiB is copied into the kernel's files as `<file name>` (`video/mp4`), with its poster as `<poster name>` (`image/jpeg`), and `fileId` is the video's copy's id and `posterFileId` the poster's, else both `null`. kvvideo's workspace collection `players` holds `{ path, fileId, posterFileId, createdAt }` (index `path`): a new copy of a path unlinks the old ones, and past 10 the oldest copies are unlinked and their rows deleted.

## 16.9 The player card

`compose`'s connector entry has `card: 'kvvideo.player'` (ADR 0082, 13), so a successful call shows kvvideo's component with `{ payload, output }`:

- the file's path and the video's length (`m:ss`), and the other sizes' paths;
- a `<video>` with controls, `preload="metadata"`, its poster from `/api/files/<posterFileId>?workspaceId=<the tab's workspace>`, at most 360 px high, from `/api/files/<fileId>?workspaceId=<the tab's workspace>`;
- with `fileId` `null`, or when the video can't load, "This video can't play here. Open `<file>` in the workspace." (`kvvideo.player.unavailable`) in its place.

The component is `kvvideo/web/components/player.vue`, built as kvcoder's are (plan 08 §8.7), with logical CSS and kvwebui's variables.

## 16.10 `review`

`kvvideo.video.review { file }` → `{ durationMs, loudness: { integratedLufs, truePeakDb } | null, problems: [{ kind, atMs, lengthMs, reason }], images: [{ fileId, name }] }` (ADR 0115, 20 and 48). `file` is an `.mp4` in the workspace; `loudness` is `null` for a video without sound, which has no `silence` or `loudness` problem.

- **Problems**, in time order: `silence`, sound below −50 dB for 3 s or more (`silencedetect`), but for one that reaches the video's end and lasts at most 5 s; `black`, a black picture for 0.5 s or more (`blackdetect`); `frozen`, a picture unchanged for 3 s or more (`freezedetect`, ADR 0120, 1); `loudness`, an integrated loudness outside −15 to −13 LUFS or a true peak above −1 dBTP (`ebur128`), at `atMs` 0 for the whole video. Each has a `reason` in English with what was measured and its bound (ADR 0120, 23): "No sound for {s} s.", "A black picture for {s} s.", "The picture doesn't change for {s} s.", "The integrated loudness is {lufs} LUFS, outside −15 to −13 LUFS.", or "The true peak is {db} dBTP, above −1 dBTP." (both, joined by a space, when both are off), with one decimal.
- **Contact sheets.** The frames are taken, from the video's sidecar, at the middle of each scene, 400 ms after each transition, and at the middle of each caption line; without a sidecar, every 5 s. Frames within 500 ms of an earlier one are dropped; past 96, 96 are kept, evenly spread. Each sheet is 3 columns × 4 rows of frames 480 px on their longer side, each labelled with its time (`m:ss.s`), as JPEG, quality 85; at most 8 sheets. They are stored in the kernel's files as `review-<n>.jpg`, and listed in `images`, which the model sees (plan 08 §8.4). kvvideo's workspace collection `reviewSheets` holds `{ fileId, createdAt }`; past 24, the oldest are deleted with their files.

## 16.11 Errors

`kvvideo/PROGRAM_MISSING`, `kvvideo/BROWSER_MISSING`, `kvvideo/STEP_FAILED`, `kvvideo/NARRATION_NOT_SET`, `kvvideo/NARRATION_KEY_MISSING`, `kvvideo/NARRATION_FAILED`, and `kvvideo/RENDER_FAILED`, each with `kvvideo.errors.<CODE>` in `en` and `ar`. A `check` reports what is missing in its answer and fails for none of them.

## 16.12 Testing

- Pure parts (captions and their times from an alignment, pronunciations, the ASS file in each style, the camera's stops and its expressions, the transitions' offsets, each scene's and the final pass's ffmpeg arguments, the cache's key, addresses, the page's routing, the font name table, the kill per OS) are unit tests.
- The commands run with `createTestKernel` and kvcoder's `runConnector`, with stand-in `ffmpeg`, `ffprobe`, and `piper` programs named by the `kvvideo.programs.*` settings, which record their arguments and write their outputs.
- The two services are tested with a stand-in `fetch`; no test reaches the network.
- `record` (a website and a page, a desktop and a phone, with a narration, a page's animation held until the capture starts, a page drawn frame by frame with a CSS animation, a script on `requestAnimationFrame`, a timer, a three.js scene, and sound cues, the same at two calls, and failed loads), `look` (a page's animation and script at each moment, the same at two calls), kvvideo's address (the kit, the brand's CSS, logo, and font, the libraries), the drawn frames and callouts, and `compose`'s final pass run end to end with the installed Chrome and the ffmpeg on the PATH against local pages, in the e2e project.
- The card is mounted in `happy-dom`.
- By hand: the KVCare promotion of ADR 0120 made again with `/video`, in the owner's voice, and looked at against its faults; a real ffmpeg (with libass) composes a video with a title page, a window and a phone recording of a website, a person's image and a licensed one from the web, the camera, overlays, every transition, sound effects, and English and Arabic captions in each style; `review` finds a planted silence and black frame; each service narrates once with the owner's keys (OpenAI's `instructions` and `speed`, ElevenLabs's timestamps); the owner records a scene with `ask record` in the browser, the desktop app on each OS, and VS Code.
